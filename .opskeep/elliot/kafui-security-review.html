Overview

Kafui (said kah-FOO-ee), one AI agent that answers JayFoods customers on the business WhatsApp number and in a live chat on the website, takes juice orders and bookings priced only from the admin catalogue, hands off to staff, recognises the owner, and prepares invoices for the owner.

The problem

JayFoods takes orders by hand on WhatsApp and phone, so enquiries outside working hours wait, prices are quoted from memory, and bulk enquiries from offices, events and shops are slow to answer and easy to lose.

Evidence: The business number was just moved to the WhatsApp Business Platform with no one answering it; the site's bulk path is a prefilled WhatsApp message with no reply automation; Caleb's brief (23 Oct 2026).

The bet

We believe customers will order and book on WhatsApp and the website chat if replies are instant, prices are exactly the admin's, and a person is one message away, so we are betting about a week of build and testing, AI provider usage costs, and the risk of an AI agent writing orders that staff must confirm that this produces more orders and bulk enquiries captured and answered within a minute, at any hour, with no pricing mistakes. We will watch agent-taken orders per week, share of conversations handed off, median first-reply time, and number of price corrections staff make.

Outcomes

  • Every WhatsApp and website chat message gets a correct reply within a minute, day or night
  • Orders and bookings arrive in the admin with items, quantities, delivery details, name and phone, priced from the admin list
  • A customer who asks for a person, or whom the agent cannot help, reaches staff, who can reply from the admin
  • The owner can ask for an invoice or a sales summary from their own WhatsApp and gets it
  • No customer can see another customer's orders, and no one can change a price or discount through the chat

What customers and the owner feel

  • Push: Missed and slow replies, especially bulk enquiries; prices and delivery fees given inconsistently by hand
  • Pull: An instant, correct answer with real prices, a clear order summary and a reference number, on the channel customers already use
  • Anxiety: Customers worry about a bot getting prices or orders wrong, about being stuck with no person to talk to, and about their number and address being shared; the owner worries about wrong prices, fake orders and other people seeing sales figures
  • Habit: Customers message or call the number and expect a person; staff write orders down by hand

Paths not taken

  • Gemini and DeepSeek in the provider chain. Caleb chose Lisa's four providers (CHG-001)
  • ElevenLabs Agents hosting the conversation (Lisa's WhatsApp voice setup). Caleb chose against it; the model would run outside our server and prices would depend on tool wiring in a third-party dashboard
  • Twilio as the WhatsApp provider (Lisa's original transport). The number is already registered directly with Meta; Twilio adds a per-message fee, a second account and a sender migration for no new capability. The sender sits behind one class so Twilio can replace it later
  • Free-form model replies with prices written by the model. Prices could be invented; all money figures come from server-side tools instead
  • A separate WhatsApp-only price list. Two price lists drift; the admin catalogue stays the single source

Kafui's tools and human gates

One brain for both channels. Money is computed only by tools from the admin catalogue, orders land as pending for staff, and a person is one message away.

Customer tools (WhatsApp and website)

  • get_menu flavours, sizes, prices, bulk tiers, zones, hours
  • quote_order server-made summary with a quote id
  • place_order only from a quote shown in this chat
  • create_booking (meaning set in DEC-001)
  • my_orders own orders only
  • request_invoice own order, sent to the owner
  • handoff to a person

Owner tools (listed WhatsApp numbers only)

  • sales_summary for a date range
  • list_orders, list_bookings
  • create_invoice PDF for any order, sent to the owner
  • Nothing that cancels, refunds, changes prices, stock or settings

Incidents and recovery

  • Backup: Existing authenticated admin backup plus a daily off-server copy kept 30 days (CTL-REC-002).
  • Restore: Replace database/jayfoods.sqlite with the chosen backup, run migrate.php; rehearsed once before go-live.
  • Rollback: Turn the agent off in the admin, then redeploy the previous tagged release; the new tables are additive and safe to leave.
  • Incident response: Kill switch, rotate Meta token, app secret and AI keys, review the action log, notify the owner, restore if data changed (CTL-REC-001).

DEC-001

What do 'bookings' mean for JayFoods?

Decided (Caleb, 23 Oct 2026): event and bulk-supply bookings for a future date (date, time, venue, number of guests, juices wanted), saved for staff to confirm and quote, plus 'call me back' requests. No calendar slots or deposits

Owner: Prince Caleb with the JayFoods owner

DEC-002

Which WhatsApp numbers are the owner, and what may the owner do from WhatsApp?

Decided (Caleb, 23 Oct 2026): one or two listed numbers, entered in the admin (numbers to come from Caleb); owner tools are read-only (sales summary, order and booking lists) plus invoice creation; no cancelling, refunds, price or stock changes from WhatsApp

Owner: JayFoods owner

DEC-003

Which AI providers run the agent, on whose account and with what spend limit?

Decided (Caleb, 23 Oct 2026, revised by CHG-001): Lisa's chain, Anthropic then OpenAI then OpenRouter then Groq, each a fresh attempt with its own time limit and an optional backup model, then the fixed reply and a staff handoff if all fail; keys on the client's own accounts (or Caleb's, billed through Opskeep), with a monthly spend cap at each provider

Owner: Prince Caleb

DEC-004

Data protection: does the owner treat Act 843 as applicable, will they register with the Data Protection Commission, and do they accept 90-day transcript retention and the privacy notice changes?

Decided (Caleb, 23 Oct 2026): Act 843 treated as APPLICABLE; 90-day transcript retention; privacy notice updated; not legal advice

Changes waiting for approval

CHG-001, modified, proposed

AI provider chain changed to Lisa's: Anthropic, then OpenAI, then OpenRouter, then Groq, each with an optional backup model and its own time limit (20 s, OpenRouter 30 s), whole chain at most 90 s. Gemini and DeepSeek removed.

Before: Providers per DEC-003 (Gemini, Anthropic, Groq, OpenAI, OpenRouter, DeepSeek in that order) sharing one 25 s budget

Now: Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Groq in that order, main then backup model per provider, 20 to 30 s per attempt, 90 s cap

Why: Caleb (23 Oct 2026): match the prince-web-app setup and give every provider a fallback so one slow provider cannot starve the others

Impact: Two fewer processors receive chat text. A message can now cost up to five model attempts and take up to 90 s before the fixed reply, so the cost cap per message is higher; per-sender message caps, spend limits at each provider and the no-retry-after-a-tool-acted rule are unchanged
